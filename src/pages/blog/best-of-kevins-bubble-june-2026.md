# Best of Kevin's bubble - June 2026

## Links

### [It doesn't matter if it works](https://henry.codes/writing/it-doesnt-matter-if-it-works/)

The one "new" thing I learned from this article was an effect of the industrial revolution I never thought about. "Machines" took the highly specific skils and knowledges of experts and automated them, making these experts "useless". And therefore "de-skilled" the workforce overal. As that specific knowledge/skill was not necessary to have anymore. Which also allowed companies to pay less for workers that were able to handle these machines. It makes me once more wonder what our work life will look like in a few years when the AI boom has settled.

### [LLMs and performative productivity](https://joshcollinsworth.com/blog/productivity)

I need to be honest with you, by the time I write this I haven't finished reading the article yet, because it's way too long for my typical "blog attention span" 😅
Still it triggered a thought.

This month I started playing around with cursor and Claude a bit more. I specifically worked on a feature where I needed a backend, but the backend developers were all busy with other stuff, so instead I let one of my dear colleagues help me with having a good mental model of what should happen in the backend and then prompted away with nothing but hopes and prayers in my backpack. So far, it looks good enough by I started realizing something while chatting with Claude. It felt like I was guiding a junior through the codebase, stopping them when they were about to do something unnecessary, adjusting their direction where I felt it was necessary, and other than that let them do and then look at their result in gitlab. And this is where I feel similar to the author, working with the chatbot felt like I was helping a junior finish their task, while in reality it was doing my task. Which by the way didn't stop at backend code generation. I hooked up an atlassian MCP and gave it the necessary Test Rail and gitlab CLI tools so it could do all kinds of stuff I usually need to do when finishing a task, but with which I often have a hard time getting myself to do. And just like the author it almost felt addictive, I wanted to play the chatbot game further. Very, very weird. I am excited to see where this goes, and how my usage of the bot develops over time. We'll see.
